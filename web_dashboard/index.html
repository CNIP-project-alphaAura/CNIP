Network Performance Analysis of a University E-Learning System

When hundreds of students attend live classes, stream video lectures, check portals, and submit online exams simultaneously, network bandwidth gets choked. This project simulates and analyzes network congestion, delay, and packet loss using NS-2 (Network Simulator 2), and demonstrates how algorithms like Go-Back-N and Leaky Bucket ensure reliable and smooth communication.

📋 Problem Statement & Purpose

University e-learning systems provide four core services: Online Classes, Video Lectures, Learning Management System (LMS), Student Portal, and Online Examination Portal.

🟢 Low Load (10 Students)

Normal operating hours. Network runs smoothly with under 0.6% packet loss and high delivery ratio (99.5%).

🟡 Medium Load (50 Students)

Peak lecture hours. Router queue fills up, causing slight delay and ~5.5% packet loss as students stream lectures.

🔴 High Load (100 Students)

Exam submission deadline. Bottleneck link saturates completely, causing queue overflow and >17% packet loss without traffic shaping.

🔄 TCP vs UDP Protocol Comparison

How reliable delivery compares against real-time streaming under student traffic

TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)

Reliable · Connection-Oriented

TCP guarantees that every single packet is received intact. If a packet is lost, the sender halts and retransmits it.

  • ✅ 100% Reliable Delivery: Perfect for assignments & exam papers.
  • ✅ Congestion Avoidance: Automatically slows down when network clogs.
  • ⚠️ Slight Delay: Waits for acknowledgements (ACKs) before proceeding.
  • 🏛️ Used By: Learning Management System (LMS) & Online Exam Portal.

UDP (User Datagram Protocol)

Fast · Connectionless

UDP sends packets immediately without waiting for confirmation. If packets drop, it keeps playing the video without pausing.

  • ⚡ Ultra Low Latency: Immediate delivery without round-trip waits.
  • 📺 Smooth Streaming: Live classes continue playing smoothly.
  • ⚠️ No Retransmission: Dropped packets are permanently lost.
  • 📹 Used By: Video Lectures, Zoom / Live Classes, Announcements.

🪟 Sliding Window Algorithm Demonstration

How network senders transmit multiple packets without waiting for individual ACKs

In simple communication (Stop-and-Wait), the sender sends 1 packet and waits for an acknowledgement (ACK) before sending the next one. This is very slow!

With Sliding Window, the sender can transmit a whole "window" of packets (e.g. 4 packets) consecutively. As each ACK arrives from the receiver, the window slides forward to transmit the next packet immediately.

🔁 Go-Back-N ARQ Demonstration

What happens when a packet is dropped, and how the sender recovers

In Go-Back-N, when a packet is lost in transit, the receiver refuses to accept subsequent packets out of order. When the sender's timer expires, it "goes back" to the lost packet and retransmits it and all following packets in the window.

🪣 Leaky Bucket Traffic Shaping Demonstration

How video traffic bursts are converted into a steady, reliable stream to eliminate congestion

Students starting high-definition video lectures create sudden, heavy traffic spikes (4 to 5 Mbps). A normal router queue overflows, dropping more than 50% of the packets.

The Leaky Bucket algorithm absorbs these bursts into a buffer (the bucket) and releases packets into the network at a steady, constant leak rate (1 Mbps), preventing queue overflow and achieving 0% packet loss!

📋 Summary of Results & Technical Glossary

Plain English explanations of all key terms and complete simulation observations

Everyday Definitions of Technical Terms

🚀 Throughput (Network Speed)

The total volume of data successfully delivered to students per second, measured in Kilobits per second (Kbps) or Megabits per second (Mbps).

⏱️ End-to-End Delay (Waiting Time)

The time it takes for a data packet to travel from the university server to the student's computer, measured in milliseconds (ms).

❌ Packet Loss Rate (Lost Data)

The percentage of packets that were dropped along the way because a router's memory buffer became completely full.

📦 Packet Delivery Ratio (Success Rate)

The percentage of all transmitted packets that arrived safely at their destination: (Received / Sent) × 100.

Key Findings from NS-2 Simulations

Experiment Observed Result Engineering Solution
Student Scaling (10 to 100) Packet loss surged from 0.5% up to 17.1% as student count hit 100. Increase bottleneck router buffer size and link capacity.
TCP vs UDP TCP retransmitted lost exam/LMS packets; UDP dropped video frames without pause. Use TCP for exams and assignments; use UDP for live video classes.
Leaky Bucket Drops reduced from 57.7% down to 0% once bucket capacity reached 100 packets. Place a Leaky Bucket traffic shaper at the campus video streaming gateway.
Go-Back-N Window Window size W=16 offered optimal balance between pipelined throughput and low retransmission overhead. Set TCP congestion window size dynamically based on link quality.
